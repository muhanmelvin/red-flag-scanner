Recon Red-Flag Scanner · v1.0

User Guide

Run the tenant-side checklist on a landlord's operating-expense reconciliation and get back a ranked list of red flags — each with its arithmetic and a finding-letter sentence. Everything runs in your browser; nothing you load is sent anywhere.
Synthetic demo data only (Maplewood Commerce Center). No login, no server, no AI in the numbers.

1. Open it

  • Local: run npm run dev in the repo and open http://localhost:5173 — or open dist-single/index.html after npm run build:single (works offline, no server).
  • Hosted: the Cloudflare / GitHub Pages URL once deployed, or the quick-share artifact link.

The page lands on MW-B already scanned. There is nothing to set up and nothing to sign in to.

2. Pick a package

Four cards sit at the top of the page. Click one and the scan runs instantly; the URL hash (for example #MW-B) is shareable.

3. Read the summary band

  • Estimated tenant impact — the sum of every quantified overcharge (the "est. impact" figures). Labelled estimated on purpose: it is what the checks can price, not a settlement number.
  • High / Review / Info — counts by severity:
    • ▲ High a lease-terms or arithmetic violation the engine can price.
    • ● Review a pattern that warrants a document request (a swing, a vanished line, an identical amount). These carry an "at stake" figure, not an impact, and are never summed.
    • ○ Info context, or a priced finding that fell below your materiality threshold.
  • Checks run — how many of the 12 checks could run on this package. The ones that couldn't are listed under the findings with the reason (e.g. "no cap in the lease").
  • Shape of the report — one bar per check, RF-01 … RF-12, segmented by severity. A glance tells you whether this is a cap story, a fee story or an allocation story. n/a means the check did not run.

4. Work the findings

Findings are ranked: High first, then by dollar impact. Each card shows severity, check ID, year(s), the line concerned, and the dollar figure. Click a card (or press Enter on it) to expand:

  1. Narrative — two or three sentences in finding-letter register: what the statement did, what the lease says, what Tenant requests.
  2. Show the working — the arithmetic line by line (base, rate, ceiling, billed, excess, tenant share, impact). For cap findings the full multi-year schedule follows.
  3. Implements — which audit concept the check encodes (e.g. Burke, Cap Traps: cap-on-cap).
  4. Read with — links to related findings (a vanished line ↔ the new line ↔ the migration that explains both).
  5. Copy finding — copies narrative + working as plain text, ready to paste into a letter or workpaper.
Try this. On MW-B, open the top RF-06 finding and read the "Landlord's stated cap" row — the engine names how the landlord got its number (prior year's billing grown 5%).

5. Scan settings

Scan settings (top right of the Findings zone) opens three knobs; the scan re-runs as you change them.

Try this. Raise materiality to 400 on MW-B: the RF-04 migration finding ($357.56) drops to Info — a live demonstration of why materiality is a tenant decision, not a default.

6. Upload your own statement

  1. Click Upload your own. Drop an .xlsx, .xls or .csv, or choose a file. (A .json package in the canonical schema skips straight to the scan.)
  2. Step 1 — Confirm the columns. The page detects the header row and guesses each column's role: Label, Section, Bucket, or Amount for year ____. Fix anything it guessed wrong; give every amount column its year.
  3. Step 2 — Confirm the lines. A preview table lists every expense line with its section, bucket (controllable / non-controllable / unknown) and a Fee? flag. Rows with a label but no amount are treated as section headers; rows labelled Total / Subtotal are skipped.
  4. Step 3 — Lease terms. Premises sf, the landlord's denominator, billed share %, the lease's stated share % if any; tick cap / fee / gross-up and fill in the few fields each needs. Blank means "not stated" — the corresponding checks report themselves as not run rather than guessing.
  5. Scan this statement. The result appears as a new card; adjust a bucket or a lease term and scan again.
Best input shape. One label column and one amount column per year, headed by the year (download the template from the upload panel). Money strings like $1,234.56 and (1,250.00) are understood.
Privacy. The page ships a Content-Security-Policy that forbids any network connection, so the file physically cannot leave the browser.

Test-drive it with a synthetic package from Recon Foundry

If the file you want to try is a client's and cannot leave the office, forge one instead. Recon Foundry is the sibling app in this family: give it a seed and it forges a whole reconciliation package for an invented property — workbook, general ledger, billing statement, tax and insurance backup, amortization schedule, tenant ledger and the lease — where every figure ties to the cent except exactly where you told it to cheat.

  1. In Recon Foundry, tick the schemes you want planted and press Download for the Scanner (.json).
  2. Come back here, open Upload your own, and drop that file on it. There is no mapping step: the file is already in the shape this engine reads, so it goes straight to the scan.
  3. The package keeps its own name, property and tenant on the card, and the answer key over there says what should have been found.
The JSON path sees more than a workbook can show. An XLSX or CSV upload carries expense lines and little else: the landlord's own cap computation, a line's capital block, whether a gross-up was applied, the statement's subtotals and the tax backup are not columns in a spreadsheet. So RF-11 reports itself as not run — there are no subtotals or tenant amounts to recompute — RF-13 reports itself as not run for want of a tax backup, and RF-10 only runs where the statement says a gross-up was applied. RF-06 and RF-09 still run, but see less: the ceiling can be rebuilt from the lease with nothing stated to compare it against, and a lump that looks capital can be flagged without the amortization arithmetic being reproducible. On one forged package the difference was 12 checks run, 11 findings and $14,152 through the JSON, against 10 checks, 7 findings and $11,752 through what a workbook can carry. It is a limit of the input, not of the checks.
One planted scheme is findable only through the paper. Recon Foundry can plant a kept tax refund — the landlord wins an assessment appeal, receives the money and never credits it to the reconciliation. RF-13 finds it, but only because a ReconPackage JSON can carry the collector's account: the levy as issued and the credits granted against it. Upload the same package as a workbook and the scheme is invisible, because a reconciliation statement does not contain the fact that a refund exists. Worth knowing before you conclude a package is clean because this page said so — check the Checks not run footer for RF-13.

7. The thirteen checks at a glance

IDNameWhat it asks
RF-01Year-over-year varianceDid a line move ≥ threshold, and is the tenant-level change material?
RF-02New categoryWhat appeared this year that wasn't there last year? (High if it's a fee, or a non-controllable line under a cap)
RF-03Category vanishedWhat disappeared — and did something of similar size appear elsewhere?
RF-04MigrationDid a line change bucket/section, and does the move let cost escape the cap?
RF-05Round numbersIs a pool figure suspiciously round?
RF-06Cap complianceRebuild the ceiling from the lease; was the pool billed above the lesser of actual and cap? Is billing growing exactly +X% a year?
RF-07FeesDoes the fee reproduce as rate × permitted base? Fee on fee? Stacked fees? Fee outside the cap when the lease puts it inside?
RF-08Pro-rata shareBilled share vs stated; vs numerator ÷ denominator; shrinking denominator; pool × share vs tenant total
RF-09Capital & amortizationDoes the installment reproduce from cost ÷ months × months in service? Still billed after end of life? Lumps that should amortize?
RF-10Gross-upFixed costs grossed up? Variable costs grossed beyond the target occupancy?
RF-11Arithmetic tie-outDo the landlord's subtotals, tenant lines and balance due add?
RF-12Identical amountsSame amount to the cent two years running?
RF-13Tax backup vs. statementDoes the tax line exceed the collector's levy net of the credits granted? (Needs the tax backup — not run without it)

8. What the scanner deliberately does not do

  • It does not guess. If the lease term or statement field a check needs is missing, the check reports itself as not run with the reason.
  • It does not use AI for any number. Every figure comes from a deterministic engine; same input, same output.
  • It does not settle. "Estimated tenant impact" is what the checks can price; overlapping findings (e.g. a fee inside a pool that is also over its cap) are each priced on their own premise.
  • It never sends data anywhere. There is no server to send it to.
